# M016: wphase parity: selectable statistic and workflow article

**Status:** done (2026-10-07, PR #11 https://github.com/jmgirard/bsync/pull/11)

**Goal:** Bring `wphase()` to the level of the other estimators: a selectable aggregate statistic that its surrogate test matches, and a workflow article.

**Outcome:** `wphase()` and `wphase_surrogate()` take `statistic = c("mean_plv", "peak")`. `"mean_plv"` is the default and returns the same values as before. `"peak"` is the per-window maximum PLV across lags, averaged over windows, which is the definition `wcc_aggregate()` uses. One helper, `wphase_aggregate(plv, window_id, statistic)`, serves the observed data and every surrogate. `create_wphase_df()` returns `window_id` as grid positions from before the time mapping, so tied timestamps cannot merge windows. `settings$statistic`, `names(aggregate)`, `glance()`, and the print labels ("Mean PLV" / "Mean Peak PLV", shared through `wphase_agg_label()`) carry the choice. `@md` was added to the `wphase()` roxygen block. New article `vignettes/wphase-workflow.Rmd` covers a 0.5 Hz rhythm with shared tempo wander, band-pass 0.3-0.7 Hz (`gsignal::butter` + `filtfilt`), the 1000-surrogate circular test with `lag_max`, the peak statistic, optima, and plots. Every result in its prose is inline R. It shows PLV optima correlating 0.50 with the simulated lead against 0.98 for relative phase at lag 0, so it has no leadership-index section. The article is linked from README, `bsync.Rmd`, `choosing-parameters.Rmd`, the other workflow articles, `_pkgdown.yml`, and `wphase()` `@seealso`. Tests in `test-wphase.R`: an explicit-loop peak oracle on two cases (default increments, and increments 2/3 with asymmetric lags and tied `time`), a matched-null check over a 3-column surrogate matrix, unknown-statistic errors, and settings/glance/print labels. DESIGN §9 has a new row. Absorbed the candidate rows "wphase workflow vignette" and "Selectable wphase aggregate statistic".

**Decisions:** none cross-cutting. Plan choices: band-pass in the article rather than a new `smooth_signal()` method, and `"peak"` as the second statistic to match wcc.
