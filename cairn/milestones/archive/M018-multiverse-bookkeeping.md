# M018: Multiverse bookkeeping: repeated cells, surrogate counts, circular skips

**Status:** done (2026-10-11, PR #13 https://github.com/jmgirard/bsync/pull/13)

**Goal:** Make `synchrony_multiverse()` and `autotune_wcc()` report each specification once, with the surrogate count and parameters it used, and skip circular cells on short series instead of stopping.

**Outcome:** `synchrony_multiverse()` drops a cell whose `window_size`, `lag_max`, `window_increment`, `surrogate_method`, and `statistic` repeat an earlier cell (lag cap, rounding, or repeated argument values). It keeps the first cell, gives one `bsync_duplicate_cells` message, and stores the count in `settings$n_dropped`. `autotune_wcc()` gives that message once per call, and its `n_cells_gated` counts kept cells only. Kept-cell values equal main at ab7c509 (`test-multiverse-pins.R`, difference 0). The grid has an integer `n_surrogates` column (0 for a cell not computed), `print()` shows "up to N (fewest m)", and `glance()` adds `n_surrogates_min`. For wcc and wdtw, when n <= 4 * the largest grid lag, circular cells that fit their window but have no valid shift are skipped with `bsync_circular_skipped`, and the circular matrix uses the largest remaining circular lag. `autotune_wcc()` records `increment_pct`, `statistic`, and `surrogate_method` of the winner. `build_surface_grid()` returns an integer `n_r`, and the `window_size`, `window_increment`, and `n_windows` grid columns are integer. NEWS has a "Multiverse results" section, and `choosing-parameters.Rmd` explains its 9-cell grid. Absorbed five candidate rows: delivered surrogate count, duplicate cells from the lag cap, autotune winning-cell fields, circular surrogates at the minimum length, and window counts as doubles.

**Decisions:** none cross-cutting. Dropping repeated cells was the fix chosen at the 2026-10-07 question set. The per-cell column was chosen at the M018 question set.
