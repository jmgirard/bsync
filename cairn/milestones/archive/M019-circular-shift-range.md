# M019: Circular surrogates in the multiverse: a shift range per cell

**Status:** done (2026-10-11, PR #14 https://github.com/jmgirard/bsync/pull/14)

**Goal:** Give each circular cell of `synchrony_multiverse()` its own range of valid circular shifts, with distinct shifts only, and skip a cell that has too few shifts to reach p <= .05.

**Outcome:** In `synchrony_multiverse()` (and `autotune_wcc()`), a circular cell shifts `y` by m to n - m samples (D-005). For wcc and wdtw, m is `max(1, 2 * lag_max)` for the cell's own lag. For wgranger, m is the window. Before, the wcc and wdtw cells shared the range of the grid's largest lag, and wgranger used `2 * round(n / 4)`, which stopped with an error or left 2 or 3 shifts. One matrix is drawn per distinct m, in the circular slot of the method order. A cell uses `min(n_surrogates, k)` distinct shifts (k = n - 2m + 1), and the grid's `n_surrogates` column records the count. A cell that fits its window with k < 19 is skipped with `bsync_circular_skipped` (fields `window_size`, `lag_max`, `n_shifts`), also when fewer than 19 surrogates are requested. This replaces M018's skip at no valid shift. The new internal helpers are `circular_shift_matrix()` and `draw_circular_shifts()`. `generate_surrogate_circular()` is unchanged, pinned against c13ffca. A single-lag wcc grid matches c13ffca exactly (hexadecimal pins), and the two-lag pins were re-derived. NEWS, the multiverse, autotune, and `glance()` docs, and the autotune skip message describe the rule. Absorbed two candidate rows: Granger circular surrogates in the multiverse, and the shared circular matrix with few shifts.

**Decisions:** D-005 (shift range per cell, one window for Granger, skip below 19 shifts). Milestone-local: the minimum shift is floored at 1 for lag-0 cells (1-sample windows), which still meets D-005's bound.
