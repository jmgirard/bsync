- **Branch/PR:** m018-multiverse-bookkeeping

## Goal

Make `synchrony_multiverse()` and `autotune_wcc()` report each specification once, with the surrogate count and parameters it used, and skip circular cells on short series instead of stopping.

## Scope

**In:**
- Drop cells that repeat another cell in samples, keep the first, and announce the count. The lag cap at `floor(window / 2)` and the rounding of seconds to samples make such repeats.
- Add a per-cell `n_surrogates` grid column. `print()` says "up to" when cells differ. `glance()` gains `n_surrogates_min`.
- For wcc and wdtw on a series too short for the largest lag of the grid, skip the circular cells that cannot shift and compute the rest.
- `autotune_wcc()` records the `statistic`, `surrogate_method`, and `increment_pct` of the winning cell.
- Store `window_size`, `window_increment`, and `n_windows` as integer grid columns. `build_surface_grid()` returns an integer `n_r`.
- Roxygen and NEWS.

**Out:**
- The minimum circular shift for wgranger cells, `round(n / 4)`, stops the call or leaves few shifts for most series lengths. It goes to the new `[high]` candidate row "Granger circular surrogates in the multiverse".
- Other multiverse candidate rows (pseudo-dyad cells, IAAFT `max_iter`) stay rows.
- The unclassed "Limited unique shifts" warning of `generate_surrogate_circular()` stays as on main, also when the fallback bound of AC5 triggers it.
- `select_specification()` lines cells up across dyads by row index and does not check that the grids match. A check goes to the new `[low]` candidate row "select_specification() grid check".

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] AC1: `synchrony_multiverse()` keeps one cell, the first in grid order, for each distinct combination of `window_size`, `lag_max`, and `window_increment` (in samples), `surrogate_method`, and `statistic`, with NA equal to NA. When it drops cells, it gives one cli message of class `bsync_duplicate_cells` that states the number dropped. `settings$n_cells`, the robustness summary, and the `n_cells_gated` of `autotune_wcc()` count only kept cells. `autotune_wcc()` gives the message once per call, not once per dyad. Tests in `test-multiverse.R` and `test-autotune.R` cover these cases: a wcc lag-cap repeat (80 Hz, `window_sec = c(1, 2)`, `lag_sec = c(0.5, 1)`, 3 cells stay, the message states 1), a window rounding repeat (`window_sec = c(1, 1.004)` at 80 Hz), an `increment_pct` rounding repeat, a wgranger repeat, a segment lag-cap repeat, a grid with no repeats and no message, and the autotune message count.
- [x] AC2: For the pinned grids, the kept cells equal the output of main at ab7c509 for the same call and seed in `observed`, `null_mean`, `null_sd`, `es`, `p`, and `n_windows` (tolerance 1e-10). The pinned grids are a wcc grid with a lag-cap repeat and the `"phase"`, `"circular"`, and `"segment"` methods, and a wgranger grid with the `"phase"` method and no repeats.
- [x] AC3: The grid has an integer `n_surrogates` column. It holds the number of surrogate series (matrix columns, repeated circular shifts counted) that the test of the cell used. It is `0L` wherever `n_windows` is 0. A computed cell is one with `n_surrogates > 0`. The `window_size`, `window_increment`, `n_windows`, and `n_surrogates` columns are integer for wcc, wdtw, and wgranger grids. In one call with `n_surrogates = 50`, a test asserts that a segment cell with 4 segments holds 23 and a phase cell holds 50. Tests also assert the types of these four columns for each estimator.
- [x] AC4: `print()` of a `bsync_multiverse` shows "Surrogates per cell" as the requested count when every computed cell used it. Otherwise it shows "up to <requested> (fewest <m>)". `glance()` keeps `n_surrogates`, the requested count, and adds `n_surrogates_min`, the fewest surrogates that a computed cell used. `n_surrogates_min` is `NA` when no cell was computed or the grid has no `n_surrogates` column. For a grid without the column, `print()` shows the requested count. Tests cover both print forms, both glance cases, and the print and glance of a result whose grid lacks the column.
- [x] AC5: For wcc and wdtw, `synchrony_multiverse()` does not stop because of the circular shift bound. A circular cell that has room for its window (n >= `window_size` + 2 * `lag_max`) but no valid shift (n <= 4 * `lag_max`) is skipped (NA values, `n_windows` 0) with a warning of class `bsync_circular_skipped`. A circular cell too short for its window is reported only as too short, as on main. The other circular cells use the largest `lag_max` among the circular cells with a valid shift as the shift bound. If n is above 4 times the largest `lag_max` of the grid, circular surrogates are made as on main (the circular pins of AC2). `autotune_wcc()` gives one `bsync_circular_skipped` warning per call, and the warning names the dyads. Tests cover three cases: n = 20 with `window_size` 10 and `lag_max` 5 (the cell is skipped and the call returns), a grid where a circular cell with a smaller lag still computes, and the autotune warning.
- [x] AC6: The `autotune_wcc()` result has `statistic`, `surrogate_method`, and `increment_pct` fields equal to the winning grid row, with `increment_pct` NA for a segment cell. `print()` shows them. A test over a grid with two statistics and two surrogate methods asserts that the fields equal the `best_row` of `select_specification()`. A test over a segment-only grid asserts `increment_pct` is NA.
- [x] AC7: The roxygen of `synchrony_multiverse()`, `glance.bsync_multiverse()`, and `autotune_wcc()` states the repeated-cell rule, the `n_surrogates` column, `n_surrogates_min`, the circular skip, and the new autotune fields. NEWS.md has entries for the repeated-cell drop and its effect on cell counts and `n_cells_gated`, the `n_surrogates` column, the "up to" print, `n_surrogates_min`, the circular skip, the autotune fields, and the integer column types. `devtools::test()` passes, and `devtools::check()` gives 0 errors and 0 warnings.
